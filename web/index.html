dotlet

Check whether an AI model was trained on your writing or art. Compare how it treats your work with how it treats similar work it could never have seen, and see whether the difference is bigger than chance.

Add your writing, comparable control writing the AI can't have seen, and text the AI produced. dotlet checks whether rare 8-word sequences from your work turn up in the AI's output more often than sequences from the controls.

Add your images, comparable control images the AI can't have seen, and images the AI generated. dotlet fingerprints every image (a perceptual hash that survives resizing, compression, brightness changes and mirroring) and checks whether AI images come closer to yours than to the controls.

Show a chatbot the first half of a passage and ask it to continue. A model that memorized your text tends to reproduce the real ending; for control text it can only guess. This uses the Your work and Controls files from the Text overlap tab.

What the test actually shows

No test can prove a model was trained on your work. dotlet measures whether a model treats your work differently from control work it could not have seen. It then asks how likely a difference that big would be by chance.

  • AUC: how often one of your pieces scores higher than a control piece. 0.5 means no difference, 1.0 means complete separation.
  • p-value: how often a difference this large would happen if the model treated both groups the same. The verdict uses the larger of a permutation test and a Mann-Whitney test.
  • Long verbatim matches are reported separately. A single 60-word copy can be strong evidence even when it's too rare to move the overall statistics.

Choosing controls matters most

  1. Unseen by the model. Writing or art you made after the model's training cutoff, or never published. Your own newer work is usually the best control.
  2. Matched. Same genre, style, medium, length and era. Otherwise you are measuring style, not training.
  3. Enough of it. Aim for 30 or more pieces on each side.

Before calling a match evidence, rule out the obvious: your text was in the prompt, it's widely quoted or reposted online, or the AI searched the web or was given a reference image.

More tests in the command-line tool

Some tests need model weights or an automated API run, so they live in the Python tool:

  • dotlet probe run: send completion probes to any OpenAI-compatible or Anthropic API.
  • dotlet mia: membership inference on open text models (Min-K% Prob, loss, zlib).
  • dotlet image probe: ask an image model to recreate your images from their captions.
  • dotlet image mia: denoising-loss test on Stable Diffusion-family models.

Reading a result

  • Strong evidence: the model consistently favors your work over matched controls. That's consistent with training exposure, but it doesn't show how the work got there.
  • No significant evidence isn't proof of absence. The model may have seen your work too few times to memorize it, or the sample may be small.
  • If only some of your work leaked, the AUC stays modest even when the p-value is tiny. Look at the matches themselves.
